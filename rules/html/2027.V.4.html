DRAFT 2027 WSDC Registry Event Rules

Overview

WSDC

The World Swing Dance Council (WSDC) is a non-profit organization focused on promoting and growing West Coast Swing (WCS) dance at the global level. Its mission is “To inspire a global passion for West Coast Swing and guide community growth through supporting events, dancers, and competitors with fairness, integrity, and respect.”

The WSDC respects the interests of both new and existing members of the entire West Coast Swing dance community. As a governing body of WSDC Registry Events (“Registry Events”), the WSDC Board of Directors (“Board”) has developed these Registry Event rules to ensure integrity, consistency, and fairness at Registry Events.

Registry Events

Registry Events are swing dance events organized and hosted by Professional Members (“Members”) of the WSDC and approved by its Board. Registry Events must meet standards set by the WSDC Board, demonstrating integrity and the highest ethical standards; sufficient event size, scope, and structure; and financial viability and stability. Registry Events offer a mix of social dancing, instruction, and competition, bringing together local, regional, national and international dancers.

WSDC Registry Event Rules govern the integrity of all West Coast Swing contests at Registry Events, not just Jack and Jill contests. All Registry Events must hold WSDC Jack and Jill contests. The WSDC tracks the contest results for WSDC Jack and Jill competitions at Registry Events and points awarded for placements are included in the WSDC Points Registry.

Points Registry

Developed and maintained by the WSDC, the WSDC Points Registry is the official record used to track individual competitors’ results in WSDC Jack and Jill competitions at Registry Events. The Points Registry is used to determine a competitor’s eligible skill levels for WSDC Jack and Jill competitions. The Points Registry tracks the following skill-level and age-based categories: Newcomer, Novice, Intermediate, Advanced, All Star, Champions; and Juniors, Sophisticated, and Masters, respectively.

Competitors who have earned at least one WSDC Jack and Jill competition point are assigned a unique WSDC Competitor ID number in the Points Registry.

Section 1: Registry Events Structure and Ownership

The following section outlines the minimum standards and requirements for Registry Events.

Membership and Registry Event approval/denial, rule changes, and penalties shall be at the Board's discretion.

1.1. Professional Membership

All Registry Events must be owned by Professional Members of the WSDC. Members may be individuals, groups, clubs (e.g., swing dance clubs), or organizations. Members must remain in good standing by completing the following:

  1. Follow WSDC Registry Event Rules.
  2. Be current with WSDC annual membership dues.
  3. Conduct their Registry Event(s), contests and business functions in an ethical manner.
  4. Submit their Registry Event Update Form annually – within forty-five (45) days after the close of the event – to be listed as a confirmed Event on the WSDC Events calendar.
  5. Meet their financial obligations no later than forty-five (45) days after the last day of their event.
  6. Provide fair, unbiased and untampered competitions, judging, and scoring.
  7. Enforce their stated rules, policies, and code of conduct.
  8. Submit competition results and competitor surcharge in a timely manner.
  9. Post Event Rules (including minimum age if it applies), Code of Conduct, and Tentative Schedule on their website 90 days before the event.
  10. Be compliant with the WSDC Code of Conduct; reflect the WSDC mission, values and brand; and be a member in good standing in the swing dance community. This applies to both Event Owner(s) and Event Director(s).

Loss of Registry Event status requires a one (1) calendar year waiting period before the Member(s) can submit an application for a Trial Event.

1.2. Registry Event Structure

Registry Events must occur annually on their Official Date and in the same location, and comply with the following event structure, unless otherwise approved by the Board:

  1. Registry Events must have a minimum of three (3) days of West Coast Swing dance activities (e.g., social dancing, lessons, workshops, competitions).
  2. Restricted or closed city, state, or regional events are not eligible. Trial Events will not be approved in any region designated (1) level 4 “Do Not Travel” by the US Department of State; or (2) red “Formellement Déconseillée” by the French Ministère de l'Europe et des Affaires étrangères; or (3) “Reisewarnung” by the German Auswärtiges Amt.
  3. Events must occur on their Official Date. [See Official Date Definition.]
    1. The Event may shift one (1) weekend before or after its Official Date, due to extenuating circumstances (such as venue availability) without Board approval.
  4. Registry Events must be held in (an) approved venue(s).
    1. All WSDC competition dance floors must be a minimum of 150 sq meters (1,600 sq ft), not including seating, DJ booths, stages/platforms, room projections, columns/pillars, and must be suitable for dancing (Note: concrete, marble/stone, etc., are not acceptable). Recommended competition dance floors: Sprung dance floor. A multi-layered floor – flexible hardwood top over a shock-absorbing subfloor.
    2. Lodging/guest room accommodations must be available within a 10-minute walk of the venue(s) or the event must provide a shuttle that goes to/from the accommodations suggested by the event. If the latter, shuttles must run until the end of social dancing and be of sufficient frequency and capacity needed.
    3. Venue(s) must provide full service (food and drink) and/or options must be available nearby.
    4. Non-hotel venue(s) that meet the above requirements must be approved and have additional restrictions as follows:
      1. A maximum of two (2) Registry Events may be held in the same Non-hotel venue(s) per calendar year.
      2. A Non-hotel venue owner who is also the Event Owner/Event Director may hold a maximum of one (1) Registry Event in their venue.
      3. Non-hotel venue(s) must have appropriate ambiance and staging; sufficient dance floor, lighting, sound systems, entry space, restrooms, and water, etc. suitable for a public function.
  5. All events must implement a Code of Conduct policy and post it on their website, with appropriate plans for reporting and implementation, 90 days prior to the start of the event. Sample policies are linked from the Code of Conduct section under Rules & Info on the WSDC website.
  6. Registry Events must offer a minimum of two (2) skill level WSDC Jack and Jill contests and reach a minimum of one hundred fifty (150) competitor entries, total, across all WSDC Jack and Jill contests. Jack & Jill Contest entries include all WSDC skill- and age-based contests. Individuals competing in multiple contests count per contest entry, not per person.
    1. Designated WSDC Growth Region Exception: Eighty (80) WSDC Jack and Jill competitor entries are required, and one hundred (100) attendees.
    2. If the event has fewer than the required minimum, competition results will be posted and Registry Points awarded, but the Registry Event will be placed on probation for one (1) year.
    3. During the probation year, if the event has fewer than the required minimum, competition results will be posted and the event will lose its Registry Event status.

1.3. Trial Events and New Registry Events

Event Owners/Event Directors must demonstrate that their event is viable and of sufficient size and scope to warrant Registry Event status. An approved preliminary event (“Trial Event”) must be held prior to applying for Registry Event status.

New or Existing Members who wish to hold a Registry Event must meet the following requirements:

  1. Submit Trial Event application and application fee ($100) a minimum of six (6) months prior to Trial Event. Application must include all required event criteria, including, but not limited to the following: Event Owner(s), Event Director(s), name, date, location, venue, format, competition divisions, staff, and potential conflicts as defined according to the Time/Distance Rule.
    1. Time/Distance Rule: If the proposed event change (i.e., Trial Event or change to existing Registry Event) is within 200 miles (322 km) driving distance of an existing Registry Event(s), then the proposed change and existing Registry Event(s) must be five (5) weekends apart (i.e., the events must have four (4) weekends between them).
      1. Exceptions: (All exceptions listed below require approval by the Board.)
        1. Country-specific rule: The first/only event that is located in a specific country. A majority of the Owner(s)/Event Director(s) must be resident(s) of the country.
        2. Exceptional extenuating circumstances: The Board reserves the right to approve Time/Distance Rule conflicts as part of our mission and vision (“focused on promoting and growing swing dance”). Examples are extenuating circumstances, hardship, or local market conditions that warrant approval of an exception.
  2. Receive Board approval for a Trial Event and submit an event update form to list the event on the WSDC website.
  3. Run the Trial Event, following all WSDC Registry Event Rules.
    1. Trial Events must use a Gold or Platinum level WSDC Certified Chief Judge, unless an exception is approved by the Chief Judge Committee.
    2. Trial events must use a WSDC-approved Scoring System.
    3. Trial events must have a minimum of ten (10) judges on staff, not including the Certified Chief Judge.
  4. Submit the WSDC Event Reporting form and all competition and scoring reports (e.g., list of competitors, preliminary and final contest scores, contest tabulation sheets, petitions) for verification within three (3) days of the close of the Trial Event.
    1. Points will be awarded to competitors after the Trial Event.
    2. Trial Events must submit the Competitor Surcharge fee.
  5. The WSDC Board will review the Trial Event results for Registry Event approval. Trial Events may receive one of the following designations:
    1. Approval as a Registry Event in full (i.e., no restrictions);
    2. Approval on probation (see Action Plan requirements in Section 4); or
    3. Denial. Denial may be due to any event change during the Trial Event and/or failure to meet all Registry Event rules during the Trial Event. If denied, a second Trial Event may be permitted.
  6. Approved Events must submit the annual Membership fee. The event must also complete and submit the Registry Event Update Form annually to be listed as a confirmed Registry Event on the WSDC Event calendar.
  7. A WSDC Professional Member must comply with the WSDC Code of Conduct; reflect the WSDC mission, values and brand; and be a member in good standing in the swing dance community. This applies to both Event Owner(s) and Event Director(s).

Existing Members with current Registry Event(s) who wish to apply for a Trial Event must also meet the following requirements:

  1. All of the Member’s existing events must have a minimum of two (2) years of history as Registry Events in good standing. Members may not hold two (2) Trial Events at the same time.
  2. All of the Member’s existing events must have a minimum of three (3) WSDC skill level Jack and Jill divisions, and demonstrate a history of excellence.

1.4. Existing Registry Event Changes:

Members are required to notify the Board of proposed change(s) six (6) months prior to Registry Events. Changes are subject to the written approval of the Board. Multiple changes may not be approved. Changes not approved may result in probation or loss of Registry Status. If an event changes two (2) or more of the following in the same year: 1) moving more than 100 miles (160 KM), 2) changing name, and/or 3) dates, it will be considered a new event and must go through the Trial Event process.

Changes requiring approval include:

  1. New location/Venue(s)
    1. Exception: An Event in a hotel may make a change from one location to another in the same metropolitan area without Board approval, if the new venue remains a hotel.
  2. New date
    1. The Event may shift one (1) weekend before or after its Official Date, due to extenuating circumstances (such as venue availability) without Board approval.
      1. Registry Events associated with a specific date/holiday must occur on the weekend either before or after that specific date/holiday.
      2. If an Event utilizes the allowable one-week shift before or after its Official Date for three (3) consecutive years, the WSDC Board, in consultation with Event Owner(s)/Director(s), will review the Official Date for possible change.
    2. An Event may request a one-time date change, subject to Board approval. When a one-time date change is approved, an Event must return to its Official Date (plus or minus 1 week) for at least 2 years.
    3. An Event may request an Official Date change, subject to Board approval and the Time/Distance rule [See 1.3.1a].
  3. New event name
  4. Transfer of ownership
    1. During the first two (2) years of running an event as a Registry Event, the original Owner(s) must retain at least 50% ownership, or WSDC status will not transfer to the new Owners and will not be approved.

1.5. Registry Event Hiatus:

Registry Events are permitted a one (1) year break without jeopardizing their Registry Event status. Hiatus may refer to the cancellation of the current or upcoming year. The event may be listed on the WSDC Events Calendar with a note indicating it is on hiatus. Registry Events on hiatus will keep their Official Date and location on the WSDC Events Calendar for assessing Time/Distance Rule conflicts.

A one (1) year break is automatically allowed by the Board if the following criteria are met:

  1. Members notify the Board that their Registry Event is on hiatus in writing six (6) months in advance.
    1. Failure to provide notification in the required time frame may result in the Hiatus not being granted, or loss of Registry Event status.
  2. Members maintain their WSDC Professional Membership during the hiatus (i.e., Members pay their membership fee). Members will receive no partial or full refunds for a canceled event.
    1. Exception: New Registry Events must hold two (2) Registry Events before being eligible for hiatus. This does not include the Trial Event year.

Post-hiatus:

  1. An event must complete a minimum of two (2) consecutive Registry Events before it may go on hiatus again.
  2. If no event is held in the year following the break (i.e., the event is not held for two (2) consecutive years), the event will lose its Registry Event status.

Section 2: Membership Fee, Competition Surcharges, and Competitor Results Reporting

2.1 Membership Fee

Members must pay an annual fee ($200) for each Registry Event. Membership Fee is due within 30 days after the close of the Registry Event (invoiced separately from Competitor Surcharge).

Late submission of Membership Fee may result in penalties.

2.2. Competitor Surcharge

Members must pay a Competitor Surcharge to the WSDC for all competitor entries in WSDC Jack and Jill contests.

  1. The Competitor Surcharge is $2 per competitor entry, per contest entered (WSDC Jack and Jill contests only)
  2. Competitors entering multiple WSDC Jack and Jill contests incur a Competitor Surcharge for each WSDC Jack and Jill contest (e.g., a competitor entering an Advanced as Follower, Intermediate as Leader, and Masters WSDC Jack and Jill contests would result in a $6 Competitor Surcharge).
  3. The Competitor Surcharge is due within 30 days after the close of the Registry Event (invoiced separately from Membership Fee).

Late submission of the Competitor Surcharge may result in penalties.

2.3. Competitor Results Reporting

Members must report results for all WSDC Jack and Jill competitions to the WSDC, using the WSDC Event Reporting Form, including the following information:

  1. All petitions.
  2. Finalists’ WSDC ID numbers (if assigned by the Points Registry).
  3. The number of competitors in each division, which allows the appropriate Tier level for recording points to be determined.
  4. List of all competitors registered in WSDC Jack and Jill contests

The Event Director and/or the Chief Judge shall verify contest results and the number of contestants.

Competition results are due to the WSDC at the close of the Registry Event, and no later than three (3) days after the event closes. Submission of contest results in a timely manner is required to respect our competitors and ensure the Points Registry remains current for upcoming Registry Events.

Late submission of competition results may result in penalties.

Section 3: Competition Requirements

3.1. Contest Structure/Format Requirements

  1. The Registry Event must hire sufficient judging staff to run contests and avoid conflicts of interest.
  2. Registry Events must offer a minimum of two (2) skill level WSDC Jack and Jill divisions.
    1. Events are responsible for informing competitors of the Registry Points skill levels and points awards as defined by the WSDC.
    2. Events may restrict the number of Jack and Jill contests a competitor may enter. This must be published in the Event’s rules.
    3. Events that wish to require male/Leader and female/Follower roles must be pre-approved by the Board and this restriction must be published in the Event’s rules.
    4. Events may restrict their staff from participating in Jack and Jill or other contests.
    5. While Registry Events must be open to everyone, events may set a minimum age requirement for contestants. This must be published in the Event’s rules.
  3. Registry Events may offer only one (1) WSDC Jack and Jill for each skill level or age-based contest.
    1. WSDC will record WSDC points for only one (1) Junior, one (1) Sophisticated, and one (1) Masters contest per event (see Definitions: Age-based Contests). WSDC age-based Jack and Jill contests must be open to competitors of all skill levels who meet the age-based requirement.
    2. Events must clearly state in the Event rules which contests qualify for WSDC points (e.g., WSDC Champions Jack and Jill vs Invitational or non-WSDC Champions contest).
  4. WSDC Jack and Jill competitions must have a minimum of five (5) unique Leaders and five (5) unique Followers in finals for points to be awarded and the results to be included in the Points Registry.
    1. Exception: A Tier 1 contest with only five (5) unique competitors in one role (and five (5) to ten (10) competitors in the other role) may run a preliminary round, with a cut to no less than 3 couples in the finals, at the Chief Judge’s discretion.
  5. Registry Events must use the Points Registry, this rules document, and the WSDC Skill Level Jack and Jill Advancement Chart to determine competitors’ appropriate skill levels. All dancers in a contest must be registered for their contest.
  6. A Petition Process is for competitors who wish to dance in a different skill division:
    1. Petitions can only move your skill level one (1) division, either up or down.
    2. For petitions down (primary role only):
      1. The Chief Judge, or qualified individual(s)/committee designated by the Chief Judge, may approve or deny the petition after reviewing the competitor's Points Registry record.
      2. Petitions are event-specific and not transferable. Competitors must submit a petition at their next event.
    3. For petitions up:
      1. Petitions must be submitted to the WSDC Chief Judges Committee (“CJC”) at least fifteen (15) days in advance of the event, along with two (2) videos of the competitor’s dancing. Petitions and videos must be submitted. The CJC may approve or deny the petition after reviewing the competitor’s Points Registry record and videos.
      2. Petitions will be approved for six (6) months and are transferable to other events. Competitors who obtain a point in the higher division will remain in the higher division.
    4. Petitions shall not be approved at the Event for the purpose of making a Tier (i.e., to meet the minimum number of required competitors for a Tier).
    5. Events must submit all petitions to the WSDC with the WSDC Event Reporting form.
  7. Registry Events may not offer combined WSDC skill-level Jack and Jill divisions (e.g., Advanced/All Star Jack and Jill) for registration.
    1. If a division does not meet Tier 1 requirements at registration close (e.g., there are insufficient entries in All Star JnJ), the Event Director may either combine the division with an adjacent division (e.g. Advanced/ All Star) or cancel the one that does not meet the Tier 1 requirement.
    2. When divisions are combined, points are awarded to the lower division (e.g., Advanced points for an Advanced/All Star contest), except for a combined Newcomer/Novice contest, which awards Novice points.
    3. Competitors are not permitted to dance “down” in a lower skill level contest even if the division that they qualify for is not offered. (e.g., An Advanced follower may not compete as a follower in an Intermediate if an Advanced contest is not offered at the event; A Champion leader may not dance as a leader in an All Star contest if a Champion contest is not offered.)
    4. If a contest is combined, it must be reported as a combined division on the WSDC reporting form.
  8. Contest “restarts” (e.g., due to incorrect song, clothing malfunction, sound or lighting issues) may be initiated by the Chief Judge and/or competitor, but only approved by the Chief Judge. The Chief Judge may use their discretion to allow the competitors to “restart” immediately or at a later time (e.g., at the end of the contest).
  9. Registry Events must post contest results on the Event website within 3 days of the event's close. Results must remain publicly accessible to all competitors for a minimum of 30 days.
    1. What must be included in posted results:
      1. All contest rounds (prelims, semis, finals)
      2. Final placements
      3. Score reports
    2. Results may link to the scoring system's application or website.
    3. Results may also be posted at the event itself, after awards, at the Event's discretion.
    4. Judges may be listed anonymously (e.g., Judge 1, Judge 2) on score reports, at the discretion of the Event Director, Owner, or Chief Judge.
    5. If any results are inaccurate due to scoring issues or violations, corrected results must be posted promptly.
  10. For Registry Events held over the New Year’s holiday, points will be assigned to December of the outgoing year, not January of the incoming year (e.g., points would be awarded for December 2022, not January 2023).

3.2. Competitors: Skill Levels and Points Registry

  1. Competitors are responsible for following the WSDC Registry Rules. Competitors will not receive WSDC points for placement if they fail to follow the WSDC Points Registry Rules.
  2. Competitors have one (1) unique WSDC Competitor ID number for the Points Registry.
    1. Competitors are responsible for using their WSDC Competitor ID number when registering for Jack and Jill competitions at WSDC Registry Events.
    2. Competitors are responsible for correcting their WSDC Competitor ID number errors, either at the event or with the WSDC directly.
    3. Competitors will be assigned a WSDC Competitor ID number only after they receive a point.
  3. Competitors may compete in a maximum of two (2) skill level WSDC Jack and Jill competitions, once in their primary role and once in their secondary role.
  4. Competitors may compete in multiple age-based WSDC Jack and Jill competitions for which they qualify.
  5. Competitors may compete in either their primary or secondary role in other contests.
  6. Competitors must use the Points Registry, this Rules document, and the WSDC Skill Level Jack and Jill Advancement Chart (Chart 3) to determine the skill level for their primary role.
    1. Competitors may submit petitions to dance one level “up” or “down” for their primary role only. Competitors may only submit petitions up for their secondary role. Petitions must be approved or the competitor will not receive points for placement.
    2. Competitor(s) who dance in the incorrect skill level division without a successful petition, or who are not allowed to compete at an event or in a division, must be disqualified and will not receive points for placement.
  7. Competitors will be awarded points as either a Leader or Follower. Those points are tracked separately in the Points Registry.
    1. The points required for moving up (or down) is based on the points in that role, not the combined points in their primary and secondary roles.
  8. The level a competitor is allowed to compete in their primary role determines their allowable level in their secondary role. [See Chart 4.] WSDC recommends competitors compete in their secondary role one (1) level down from the role they enter in their primary role. Competitors may dance two (2) levels down from the highest level they are allowed to compete in their primary role, with the following clarifications:
    1. Competitors with points in both their primary and secondary roles at the same skill level must select only one (1) role to compete in (either their primary or secondary role) for that event’s skill level Jack and Jill contest.
    2. When a competitor has a choice of primary role levels, the highest level the competitor qualifies to compete sets the allowable level for their secondary role, i.e. the division a competitor chooses for their primary role at the Event does not affect what level they may compete at for their secondary role.
    3. Competitors may dance two (2) levels down from their primary level (their highest competitive level), regardless of whether they choose to compete in the primary level for the Event. If they reach the “may” move up level on the WSDC Skill Level Jack & Jill Advancement Chart (Chart 3), based on their points in their secondary role, at that point they must dance only one level down from their highest allowed primary role [See Secondary Role WSDC Skill Level Jack & Jill Advancement Chart - Chart 4].
    4. Petitions down are not allowed for secondary roles.
    5. Competitors may dance in Newcomer, at events where it is offered, in the following scenarios:
      1. If they have no points in either role; or
      2. In their secondary role if they have fewer than 16 Novice points in their primary role and have zero (0) points at any level in their secondary role. Competitors who are allowed to compete in Intermediate in their primary role (no matter what level they choose to compete in at the Event) may not compete in Newcomer in their secondary role.
  9. Points are recorded only for those who place in WSDC Jack and Jill finals [see Chart 5: Points Awarded per Tier].
  10. If a competitor places twice in a division, the competitor is only awarded points for the higher placement (e.g., the contest goes straight to finals with uneven numbers; competitor places 2nd and 5th; competitor is awarded points for 2nd place).
  11. For WSDC Champion Jack and Jill contests, competitors may compete (“are allowed”) if they have one (1) Champion point or 150 All Star points and must compete (“are required”) if they have ten (10) Champion points or 225 All Star points, assuming the event offers a WSDC Champion Jack and Jill contest.
    1. Events may no longer define their Champion division by including other qualifications (e.g., alternative predefined number of Champion/Invitational points, NASDE Classic or Showcase placements), but may offer a non-WSDC Champions contest, Invitational, or show as an alternative with different qualifications for entry.
    2. Competitors with 10+ Champion points and/or 225+ All Star points (“are required” to compete in Champions) may not enter All Star contests without an approved petition, but may enter a combined All Star/Champion contest.

3.3. Contest Tiers, Points Awarded, and Competition Rounds

3.3.1. Point Tier Awards

Tiers are determined by the Chief Judge based on the number of unique competitors in each role (Leader or Follower). Dancing in finals does not guarantee that a competitor will be awarded points. Points awarded to Leaders and Followers may not be identical. The higher the number of competitors within a division, the higher the Tier, and the greater the points potential.

A division may have different tiers for Leaders and Followers. For example, a Novice contest may have 35 Leaders and 45 Followers. Leaders would receive Tier 3 points; Followers would receive Tier 4 points. The points awarded to a competitor according to their placement in finals is listed in Chart 5.

3.3.2. Competition Rounds

If the leaders and followers fall into different tiers, the number of rounds must be determined by the higher tier. The minimum number of rounds for tiers is mandatory where indicated. “Optional” and “Encouraged” rounds run at the discretion of the Chief Judge.

Registry Events are allowed to waive a mandatory competition round if the number of competitors is no more than 10% over the previous Tier’s maximum (e.g., a Tier 4 contest with 43 Followers and fewer than 39 Leaders could run a prelims and finals only). This is allowed to provide stability for contest planning.

3.4. Contest Oversight: Staff Requirements, Conflicts of Interest, Scoring

Registry Events must follow the highest ethical standards regarding the conduct of contests. Disregard of ethical standards, even if not specifically outlined in the Registry Event Rules, may result in penalties by the Board.

Rules that affect the integrity of a contest – particularly where results/scores could be impacted – apply to all contests held at the event. All efforts must be made to conduct unbiased judging and to avoid any tampering with contests and contest results.

Registry Events must follow all contest oversight requirements outlined below, unless otherwise approved by the Board:

  1. Event Director(s) and/or Owner(s) must not perform Chief Judge, Judge, or Scoring functions.
    1. Event Director(s) or Owner(s)’ immediate family (see Definitions: Immediate Family) may also not perform Chief Judge or Scoring duties.
  2. Event Director(s), Owner(s), and their immediate family; the Chief Judge, the Secondary Chief Judge (see Definitions: Secondary Chief Judge), and the Score person (or Contest Administrator with automated scoring systems) must not compete in any swing contests at the event.
    Exceptions:
    1. Children of Owners/Event Directors under the 18 years of age by close of the event (“Juniors”).
    2. Non-judged contests (e.g., exhibitions)
    3. Any contest of a different dance genre and/or sponsored and run by a separate organization. Exceptions must be authorized by the Board two (2) months prior to the event.
      1. Exceptions include: UCWDC events (wherein an Event Director, Owner, Chief Judge, and Score person may dance in a UCWDC Pro-Am West Coast Swing contest), Rising Star, NASDE, etc.. These exceptions are allowed as a ‘carve out’ to accommodate those Organizations’ rules.
  3. Chief Judge and Scoring/Tabulation functions and duties must be provided by separate individuals.
  4. Access to all contest scores is restricted to the Chief Judge, Secondary Chief Judge, Scoring Person, and the System Developer. This ensures a fair and secure process for all involved.
    1. Contest results may be issued prior to announcements to the individual(s) responsible for issuing competitor awards/compensation.
  5. Judges and scorekeepers may not take on any additional roles or responsibilities (e.g. MCs, DJs, Contest Team, photographer, etc.) during that specific contest.
    1. Exception: When a Champion’s or Invitational division is being self judged, a contestant can hold more than one role in addition to being on the judges panel.
  6. Judges must make decisions independently. Tampering or altering contest scores is not allowed.
    1. Group judging decisions (instantaneous “on the floor”), “tap out” eliminations during the contest (by an individual judge or as a group), or audience-judging are permitted only in non-WSDC Jack and Jill contests/“fun” contests (e.g., tournament-style contests, costume contests).
  7. All conflicts of interest must be identified and avoided. This is the responsibility of Event Directors, Chief Judge, and judges.
    1. Conflicts of interest are defined as the following: any Judge/Chief Judge who is judging an immediate family member, established dance partner, or current business partner (e.g., Co-Owners, Event Co-Directors), teaching partners who are promoted or contracted together at that event, or any other conflict identified by the Judge, Chief Judge, or Owner(s)/Event Director(s).
      1. The WSDC recommends staff members avoid real or perceived bias (positive or negative) between them and other members of the community, but will enforce only conflicts of interest as defined above. Staff members are responsible for providing conflicts of interest to the Chief Judge and/or Event Directors.
    2. If a conflict of interest is discovered after the contest, the scores of the judge with the conflict must be removed and replaced with the Chief Judge’s scores or the Raw Score judge’s scores and results recalculated.
    3. Judging assignments must be made to prevent conflicts. Judges/Chief Judges must recuse themselves from a judging panel to prevent conflicts.
      Exceptions:
      1. “All-American” contests or other contests with similar format, where contestants of every level are allowed and encouraged to enter.
      2. Pro-Am Jack and Jill and Pro-Am Strictly Swing competitions when judges are judging the Amateurs only and conflict of interest is with the Pro.
      3. A judge with a conflict of interest to a competitor may judge the opposite role to that competitor during Jack and Jill preliminary rounds.
      4. Self-judging contests are allowed only in Champions and Invitational contests. [See Appendix V.]
  8. Judges for WSDC Jack and Jill contests must meet the following standards:
    1. Twenty-five percent (25%) of an Event's WSDC contest judging staff must be WSDC Certified Judges [Recommended: January 1-March 30, 2027. Required: after April 1, 2027.] Exceptions will be managed by the Judging Committee.
    2. For preliminary Jack and Jill contests, a minimum of four (4) judges per role (plus Chief Judge) are required. The WSDC strongly recommends Registry Events use at least five (5) judges per role in larger (Tier 5 and 6) preliminary contests to avoid ties.
      1. Exception: An Event may assign some or all of the judges in the judging panel to score both roles for contests in Tiers 1-3. Additional songs per round may be needed. A minimum of four (4) scores per role is still required.
    3. For final Jack and Jill contests, the WSDC requires a minimum of five (5) judges (plus Chief Judge). The WSDC requires a minimum of seven (7) judges (plus Chief Judge) for finals in Tiers 5-6. Per Relative Placement scoring, an odd number of judges must be used; an even number of judges is not allowed.
    4. The Chief Judge may join the general judging panel only if there is an unforeseen/unavoidable shortage of general judges.
    5. Virtual judging is not permitted.
  9. All WSDC Jack and Jill contests must maintain the integrity of random, unbiased selection in rotating partners, pairing partners, and music selection. Assignments must be impartial and free from manipulation. Pairings and music choices may not be arranged to advantage or disadvantage any competitor.
    1. In prelims, each competitor must rotate through at least three different partners.
    2. A structured rotation is allowed, such as shifting partners by a smaller, randomly chosen number each round rather than within the full field. This number can be randomized independently for each heat or kept the same for all heats in a division.
    3. A standard choice of music categories (e.g., slow blues / fast contemporary) for finals may be offered.
    4. Exception to Randomization Rule [See Appendix].
  10. All WSDC Jack and Jill contests must use the WSDC-approved Callback system for preliminary competitions and Relative Placement scoring system for finals.
    1. Judges are allowed to use paper or an electronic system.
    2. All events must use a computerized system to calculate scores. Score Person and/or Chief Judge may do calculations for contest results by hand (“hand scoring”) only in emergency situations.
      1. Transfer of judges’ paper score sheet results to computerized systems must be audited by the Chief Judge and/or scoring team approved by the Chief Judge (i.e, verified by a separate person than the Score person inputting the data).
    3. The Chief Judge must assign unique numerical raw scores to all competitors in preliminary rounds, or assign Raw Score Judge(s) to one or both roles.
    4. Details on the Callback system standardization and requirements are listed in the Appendix. Additional information on the Callback system and Relative Placement is available on the WSDC website under Rules, see Prelim Scoring and Relative Placement, respectively).
    5. Requirements for computerized scoring systems are listed in the Appendix.
    6. For non-WSDC contests, Registry Events must publish if and for which contest(s) they opt not to use the Callback and/or Relative Placement scoring systems.

Section 4: WSDC Enforcement Policy and Penalties

Registry Event rules are enforced by the WSDC. Membership and Registry Event approval/denial, rule changes, and penalties shall be at the discretion of the Board. Unless otherwise approved, any WSDC Professional Member or Registry Event that does not comply with the WSDC Registry Event Rules will be considered in default of their membership and subject to penalties and/or loss of Registry Event status.

Event directors who hire a WSDC Certified Chief Judge will be protected from penalties for any WSDC Rule errors that fall under the Chief Judge's enforcement responsibilities.

The WSDC has developed a collaborative process for addressing issues (e.g., rule violations) that occur at Registry Events. Events may implement a mutually agreed upon and approved Action Plan to address the issue(s). The draft Action Plan addressing the issue should be submitted within ten (10) days of notice and be approved by both the Event and Board within 30-60 days. The Action Plan should address the following:

  1. What happened
  2. Who was impacted
  3. How to repair what happened (including communication plan to impacted parties)
  4. How to prevent it in the future
  5. Follow up reporting on implementation of the repair and proposed prevention (accountability follow up)

When an approved Action Plan is implemented and followed, the Registry Event is no longer considered in violation or in default. The Action Plan may be implemented by the Event Owner(s), Event Director(s), the Certified Chief Judge and/or other relevant parties.

Members and/or Chief Judges who violate Registry Event rules and/or fail to fully implement the Action Plan will be in default. Default may result in one or more of the following options:

  1. Warning or Probation
  2. Loss of Registry Event status
    1. Events who lose status are allowed to reapply for Registry Event approval, including a new Trial Event.
  3. Drop in or Loss of Certified Chief Judge status

Penalties specific to late submission of Membership fees, Competitor Surcharge, and competition results are detailed in Charts 1 and 2. Penalties for all other Registry Event rules violations are detailed in Chart 7. Members in default may reapply for WSDC membership or Registry Event status, subject to approval by the Board.

Appendix

I. Definitions

Age-based Contests:

Juniors are defined as those younger than 18 years of age by close of the event. Sophisticated age is defined as 35 years of age or older by the close of the event. Masters age is defined as 50 years of age or older by the close of the event.

Certified Chief Judge:

A Chief Judge who has demonstrated a thorough knowledge and understanding of the WSDC rules by participating in the WSDC Chief Judge Certification program and passing a final exam.

Certified Judge:

An experienced judge who has participated in the WSDC Affiliated Judges Program and has been certified by the WSDC.

Chief Judge:

The Chief Judge (or Secondary Chief Judge acting as Chief Judge) is responsible for overseeing the West Coast Swing contests at the event and ensuring the integrity of each contest. The Chief Judge must have extensive judging experience, excellent administrative skills and a proven record of competency. Only individuals who have previously served as a judge at multiple registry events and demonstrated proficiency in all aspects of judging may be appointed as Chief Judge.

  1. The Chief Judge must provide scores for each contest (except if Raw Score Judges are used).
  2. The Chief Judge must be present in the competition ballroom during contests.
  3. The Chief Judge’s scores (or Raw Score Judge’s scores) are used to break ties.
  4. The Chief Judge will remove an unusable judge’s scores and replace them with their scores (or the Raw Score Judge’s scores).
  5. The Chief Judge must review all preliminary scores to determine callbacks.
  6. The Chief Judge must review all finalists prior to posting or starting finals.
  7. A Chief Judge must review all results prior to awards being announced.
Designated WSDC Growth Region:

Designated WSDC growth regions as of 2027: Asia, Australia/New Zealand, South America/Central America, Africa, USA: Alaska, USA: Hawaii, and Canada: Nanaimo in British Columbia. Additional countries/regions may be added upon request and agreement by the Board.

Event Director(s) and/or Owner(s):
  1. Event Director(s) is any person involved in decision-making related to the hiring of judges and/or scoring staff and/or identified as the Event Director.
  2. Owner(s) is any person with a vested financial/ownership interest (e.g., shareholder) in the event.
  3. Non-profit shareholders or Board members are not considered Owners but may fall under Event Director restrictions, depending on their role.
Immediate Family:

Includes spouse, domestic partner, significant other, children, parents, in-laws, siblings.

Official Dates:

Every member event has an assigned Official Date approved by the WSDC. The dates (weekend) of the Trial Event, as approved, become the event's Official Date as a Registry Event.

Primary and Secondary Roles:

For the purpose of these rules, a competitor’s primary and secondary roles are based on the number of points in the WSDC Skill Level Jack and Jill Advancement Chart. The primary role is the one in which they have points at a higher skill level or, if they have points in both roles at their highest skill level, the one in which they have more points. The secondary role is the one in which they have points only at a lower skill level or, if they have points in both roles at their highest skill division, the one in which they have fewer points.

Raw Score Judge:

A Raw Score Judge is a separate judge, not part of a contest’s judging panel. A Raw Score Judge may be used in a contest (or multiple contests) to raw score one role. The Raw Score Judge must assign each competitor a unique numerical raw score. The Raw Score Judge does not have access to scores and does not determine heats/number of callbacks/cuts to finals/any other contest structure decisions; those duties must be performed by the Chief Judge (or Secondary Chief Judge). The Raw Score Judge is allowed to compete in other contests.

Secondary Chief Judge:

The Secondary Chief Judge has similar or the same duties and responsibilities as the Chief Judge during the Event, and functions as the Chief Judge in one or more contests. The Secondary Chief Judge is subject to the same rules and restrictions as the Chief Judge (i.e. the Secondary Chief Judge may not compete).

II. Callback System for Preliminary Competitions

The Callback System for preliminary competitions has been standardized. The following point values must be assigned to judges’ callbacks:

  1. Yes = 10
  2. Alternate 1 (Alt 1) = 4.5; Alternate 2 = 4.3; Alternate 3 = 4.2
  3. No = 0.

Scoring systems should not use more than 3 levels of Alternates (i.e., no Alternate 4). At their discretion, the Chief Judge may ask judges to use Alt 1s; Alt 1s & Alt 2s; or Alt 1, Alt 2 & Alt 3s for any specific contest. In larger contests, to minimize the potential for ties, the Chief Judge may ask for two (2) or more Alt 1s; Alt 1 & Alt 2s; or Alt 1, Alt 2, & Alt 3s.

The outcome for callbacks must be determined by the sum of the values.

  1. Only after the original calculations are complete, the Chief Judge’s/Raw Score Judge’s scores break the tie.

III. Computerized Scoring System Requirements

A computerized scoring system should do the following tasks:

  1. Place competitors within the various divisions and contests.
  2. Create preliminary and finals contest judging sheets with pre-populated information.
  3. Tabulate and rank callbacks. See Appendix II (Callback System for Preliminary Competitions) on requirements for ranking callbacks.
  4. Create callback announcement sheets.
  5. Transfer competitors to finals.
  6. Randomly assign partners (if needed).
  7. Place paired competitors in random dance order or place competitors in numerical order for “draw for dance order,” as needed.
  8. Tabulate and rank finals contests for placement results.
  9. Provide limited access to authorized users only, as appropriate (e.g., Chief Judge, judges).
  10. Prevent tampering and/or changes to results by unauthorized users.
  11. Provide results/announcement/posting sheets.
  12. Provide WSDC scoring report to WSDC.

IV. Guideline Procedure – Exception to Randomization Rule.

In some circumstances, an Event may encounter a conflict between two rules:

  • Member Events must publish a Code of Conduct with clear reporting and implementation plans. [See Section 1.2.5.]
  • All WSDC Jack and Jill contests must maintain the integrity of random and unbiased selection [See Section 3.4.9.]

In case of such a conflict, Events may narrowly depart from strict randomization in order to prevent two individuals from being paired together.

The following options are all considered best practices. Chief Judges and Event Directors should work together to decide ahead of time which is the right approach for the Event. The WSDC supports any of these approaches.

Events should document in advance the specific conditions under which Code of Conduct obligations will take precedence over randomization, and which process or options will be used. Departures from randomization should occur only in extreme circumstances when true randomization cannot be maintained. The privacy of all parties must be protected throughout.

Prelims/Semis

  1. The Chief Judge (CJ) may assign competitors with a conflict into separate heats or prevent specific pairings, when warranted by the Code of Conduct and its implementation plan.

Finals

Option 1 “On the floor” Pairings: Randomization may not be compromised in any way. Competitors have the option to withdraw from the contest. Contest proceeds with remaining competitors. [Randomization maintained.]

Option 2 Advance Pairings: Competitor A confirms with the CJ that they choose to withdraw from finals automatically if they draw Competitor B. CJ directs the scorekeeper to randomize all pairings in advance, eliminating the on-floor partner draw. If the affected competitors are paired by the system, Competitor A is withdrawn and replaced with an alternate. All competitor pairings are re-drawn. [Randomization maintained.]

Option 3: Advance Pairings: The CJ reviews all pairing reports before the contest begins. If competitors with a conflict draw one another, CJ directs the scorekeeper to re-draw competitor pairings. [Randomization compromised; Incident report must be filed to notify WSDC of rule violation, documentation, and justification.]

V. Best Practices and Guidelines for Chief Judges from the Chief Judge Committee

Guideline Procedures for Self-Judging WSDC Jack & Jill Champion & All Invitationals - Finals

  1. Prelims/Semis/Quarters
    1. Cannot be self-judged.
  2. Finals
    1. May be self-judged.
    2. The role with the most competitors must self-judge.
      1. If both roles are even, you may choose a role by random selection (toss a coin, have audience participation, draw a number, etc.).
      2. If there are fewer than ten (10) couples, both sides should judge for a better data point, but that will depend on the number of tablets available.
    3. You cannot use an even number of judges for relative placement. You must use an odd number of judges for relative placement. [See 3.4.8c.]
      1. If there is an even number of competitors, the chief judge will assign another judge to bring the total to an odd number.

Tablets/Paper Scoring

  1. If the Chief Judge wants to prevent competitors from knowing who their partner will be:
    1. The CJ may request that the scorer use the tablet system to either randomize on the spot or in advance, showing only one couple at a time (e.g., Scoring.dance, and Event Express Pro).
    2. If the tablet system cannot randomize competitors on the spot or hide competitors' pairings, the CJ may use paper scoring if they choose.
  2. If paper scoring is used, the Chief Judge or Secondary Chief Judge is responsible for auditing the contest and ensuring that when the scores are entered into the tablet system, the bib numbers, names, and scores are correct.
